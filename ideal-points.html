Ideal points, utility and the midpoint rule

Spatial models place actors and policies in a space, and typically assume that an actor likes a policy less as it moves farther from the actor’s ideal point, in any direction. The guided walkthrough starts with one actor on a policy line. It turns distance into utility, defines single-peaked and symmetric preferences, and finds the indifference set and preferred-to set of a status quo. It then turns to several voters choosing between two alternatives. With symmetric utility, the midpoint between the alternatives splits the voters into those who prefer one alternative and those who prefer the other. This dividing point is the cutpoint, and asymmetric utility moves it away from the midpoint. The walkthrough ends with a discussion of Pareto improvements and the Pareto set. Explore lets you set up your own actor or voters. Practice has cases to work through.

Throughout, the policy space runs from 0 to 10, outcomes are certain, and an actor prefers one policy to another only if it gives strictly higher utility. An actor is indifferent between two policies that give them the same utility.
